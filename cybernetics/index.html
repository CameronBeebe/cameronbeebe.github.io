Cybernetic regulation game

Ashby’s regulator

The environment plays a row (a disturbance). The regulator plays a column. A trial succeeds when the cell is a goal. The 2021 toy keeps one Pólya urn for every situation, so it never sees the row — a blind regulator. A regulator that keeps one urn per row can approach the requisite-variety bound: the best success rate possible when the disturbance is observed. The gap between the two dashed lines is the law, measured on the game in front of you.

How to read the bounds. The lower dashed line is the blind optimum: the best success rate any single column can get. The upper one is the requisite-variety bound: success if, for each row, the regulator may pick a (possibly different) column that hits a goal, when one exists. Seeing the disturbance is exactly the channel Ashby requires; it is not a cheat. Space toggles play; period steps one epoch.

Reference experiment (PROP-01). On 10×10 games, goal {3}, 10 seeds, last 5,000 of 20,000 epochs, the current Ashby class averaged 0.249 success against a blind optimum of 0.301. A disturbance-conditioned urn reached the requisite-variety bound of 0.603 on every seed. Live scores on this page use a different generator, so they will not match those figures exactly; the two bounds above the chart are computed from the game you see.
